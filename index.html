02 · Open source

Ster-Vis: a stereo depth camera for robots

Two low-cost cameras and a Raspberry Pi 5 that give a robot ZED-style depth, point clouds, laser scans and obstacle sectors over ROS 2 or HTTP.

  • 1.6% median depth error and 93% pixel coverage against ray-traced synthetic ground truth.
  • In Gazebo: zero collisions in two 180 s obstacle-avoidance runs, and a 20 × 12 m warehouse mapped at 2.7 cm median error.
  • Released through v2.1.0 under Apache-2.0, with CI on Linux, Windows, ARM64 and ROS 2 Jazzy.
  • Python
  • OpenCV
  • ROS 2
  • Raspberry Pi 5

03 · Ground robot

Stereo-Vision AMR

A 4WD robot that avoids obstacles using nothing but stereo depth from two Pi cameras, running Ster-Vis.

  • Chassis generated by a parametric Fusion 360 script, then 3D-printed, wired and brought up.
  • Reactive avoidance with an 8 s obstacle memory, a stale-depth watchdog that stops the motors, and a capped motor duty.
  • Drives itself on real hardware at about 6 fps depth on a Pi 5, with zero contacts in 180 s Gazebo runs.
  • Raspberry Pi 5
  • Python
  • Fusion 360
  • 3D printing
  • Gazebo

  • In designSecure comms · Defence

    Quanta-Link: quantum-safe drone link

    Plain MAVLink lets anyone in radio range read a drone's telemetry and send it commands. Quanta-Link sits between the flight controller and the radio at each end and encrypts and authenticates every MAVLink frame. ArduPilot, PX4 and the ground station need no changes.

    • Hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-768 (NIST FIPS 203) handshake, so recorded traffic stays secret even against a future quantum computer. Fresh keys every session give forward secrecy.
    • ChaCha20-Poly1305 on each packet, about 21 bytes of overhead, replay window, and no plaintext fallback. The air node runs on an ESP32-S3.
    • Runs over OpenHD or a separate 433 MHz SiK control link. Roadmap: spec, SITL simulation, side-by-side attack demo, then hardware.
    • C11
    • ML-KEM
    • MAVLink
    • ESP32-S3
    • ArduPilot SITL
  • In designRF · FPGA

    Anti-jam FHSS link on an FPGA

    Encryption protects what is sent; a jammer can still stop it from arriving. This link protects the transmission itself with frequency hopping that is both secret and adaptive.

    • Keyed hop sequence derived from the Quanta-Link session key, so a jammer cannot predict the next channel.
    • The radio senses the spectrum, classifies the jammer (barrage, spot, sweep, pulsed or follower) and responds by blacklisting channels, hopping faster or switching band.
    • Two levels: ESP32 with an SX1262/SX1280 radio first, then an FPGA SDR (ADALM-PLUTO, AD9363 + Zynq) for wideband sensing and dwell times shorter than a follower jammer can react.
    • FPGA
    • SDR
    • ADALM-PLUTO
    • ESP32
    • GNU Radio
